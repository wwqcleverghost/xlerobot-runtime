# v9 design: research, evidence and pre-registered expectations (2026-11-20)

中文：[v9_design_2026-11-20.zh.md](v9_design_2026-11-20.zh.md) · How to run it: [`docs/workflows/v9/`](../workflows/v9/README.md)

## 1. Why this document

The first progress-feedback batch (progdelta / progself / deltainc) failed in a way that could have been measured before training:
the progress input s was recoverable from the images (stage recognition 0.92), so the policy ignored it (2026-11-16: shifting s by
±2 stages moved the joint commands 0.1–0.6°, no more than another noise seed). It took dozens of robot trials to see that.

v9 therefore follows one rule: **a conditioning signal goes to the robot only after three checks.**

1. **Information** (before training): the signal must not be predictable from what the model already sees (images + joints).
   If it is, the model can get the same information for free and will ignore the input.
2. **Data** (before training): the training set must contain the same kind of state with different signal values and different
   actions. Otherwise there is nothing that ties the signal to the action.
3. **Intervention** (during / after training, `examples/xlerobot/gate_v9.py`): same observation, same noise, only the signal
   changed — the action must change clearly more than with another noise seed, and in the right direction.

Section 5 writes down what each model is expected to do *before* any result exists. Results are compared against it; the
expectations are not edited afterwards.

## 2. Evidence (measured in this session)

| Question | Result | Consequence |
|---|---|---|

| 54 held-out trials, stall frames (reference) | close share: baseline 0.112, progdelta 0.061, progself 0.070; progress error 0.28–0.30 stages; gain AUC 0.65–0.69 | numbers v9 must beat |
| Can good / bad (advantage) be predicted from the observation? (kNN, leave-trial-out, A对位 + A抓住) | joints AUC 0.66; image features (v6 encoder) 0.74; both 0.74; 53–58% of bad frames have ≥ 30% good neighbours | **passes information**: unlike progress (0.92), the indicator carries information the observation lacks |
| Can back vs forward be told from the observation? | position 0.47; + 1-frame velocity 0.61 | **passes information**: only the indicator says which way |
| How many recoveries after a stall exist? | stalls ≥ 2 s in A对位 / A抓住 that end: 22; followed by a grasp: 8. Demos stand still ≥ 1 s in only 83 frames | **a "stall time" input fails the data check**: 8 examples cannot teach what to do after a long stall. It stays outside the model (StallGuard), and recovery data is collected first (`STALL=ask`) |

kNN predictability is a lower bound on what a trained network can extract; the intervention gate checks the trained model itself.

## 3. Related work: what it does, how strong the evidence is, what we take, when it fails

**Advantage-conditioned policies.**
- *π\*0.6 / RECAP* (Physical Intelligence, [arXiv 2511.14759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14759)): a VLA conditioned on a binarized
  advantage indicator (threshold = 30th percentile of the value per task), trained on demos, autonomous rollouts and corrections;
  the indicator is dropped at random so classifier-free guidance can be used. Evidence: large real-world gains, but with a
  VLM-initialised value function and far more data than our 58 demos + 216 trials. *We take*: the binarized indicator, the 30%
  threshold, failures mixed in as "bad". *Our substitute for the value*: progress gained within 1 s.
- *CFGRL* (Frans et al., [arXiv 2505.23458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23458)): guidance between the conditioned and
  unconditioned policy is a policy-improvement step; a larger weight helps up to a point, then diverges. Evidence: offline-RL
  benchmarks (simulation). *We take*: the guidance weight w as a test-time knob, swept 1 / 1.5 / 2 / 3 offline.
- *When return-conditioned SL works* (Brandfonbrener et al., [arXiv 2206.01079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01079)): conditioning
  on outcomes recovers good behaviour only under strong assumptions (near-deterministic dynamics, good actions covered in the
  states that matter); it cannot invent behaviour that is not in the data. *Consequence*: guidance can only pick closing actions
  that exist near the stall states; if the data has none there, w > 1 extrapolates. This is why G1 also checks that "good"
  closes more than "bad".

**Learning from failures without imitating them.**
- *AFIL* ([arXiv 2605.08434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08434)): a success and a failure action generator on one backbone; sampling is
  pushed away from the failure one. *CCDP* ([arXiv 2503.15386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15386)): avoids previously failed samples at
  inference. *RedFlow* ([arXiv 2607.27782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27782)): groups executed chunks of similar states by estimated
  progress + proprioception, scores them and steers bad chunks toward good ones (offline, 56.7% → 74.7% on three real tasks).
  *We take*: failures as negative evidence (`adv`), the user's mask variant (`negmask`), RedFlow's grouping in the progress labels.
- Flow-matching caveat (ours): joints and progress are denoised together, so a masked frame's noisy joints still enter the
  network input. The mask removes the joint *targets*, not every influence of the failed actions.

**Progress / reward from video.**
- *SARM / RA-BC* ([arXiv 2509.25358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25358)): stage-aware progress, then sample weights by progress gained.
  Its gains came on large, noisy data; on our 58 curated demos the v6 RA-BC model tied the baseline. *We take*: RA-BC, now on the
  honest, time-decayed progress, and on rollouts where slow and stalled parts exist.
- *ReWiND* ([arXiv 2505.10911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10911)): plays successful videos backwards so a reward model learns that
  progress can fall. *We take*: reversal, but for the policy (back episodes) and only for free-space approach segments (no tape in
  hand, so the reversed motion is physically valid).
- *GVL* ([arXiv 2411.04549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04549)): VLM progress estimates; its value–order correlation is a check usable
  on any progress estimator.

**Detecting a stall and recovering.**
- *Sentinel* ([arXiv 2410.04640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04640)): separates erratic failures from "confidently not progressing" ones
  — ours is the second kind. *Rewind-IL* ([arXiv 2604.16683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16683)): flags failures by chunk-to-chunk
  inconsistency and returns the robot to a checkpointed state. *FIPER*, *FAIL-Detect*: runtime failure prediction. *We take*: a stall
  test computable live (joints in a 3° box for N s, own progress not rising) and three responses: resample, scripted rewind,
  learned retreat.
- *RaC* ([arXiv 2509.07953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07953)): human interventions that first recover, then correct; about 10× less
  data than plain demos on long tasks. *HG-DAgger* ([arXiv 1810.02890](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02890)) / *ThriftyDAgger*: the human,
  or the robot by its own risk estimate, decides when to take over. *We take*: `STALL=ask`, the robot asks for a takeover when it
  stalls, which records the recoveries the data lacks (8 today).

**Choosing among samples.**
- *V-GPS* ([arXiv 2410.13816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13816)), *RoboMonkey* ([arXiv 2506.17811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17811)):
  sample several actions, rank them with a learned verifier. *We take*: the policy's own predicted progress as a cheap verifier
  (`best_of_n`); limited by the share of good samples at stall states (section 2).

**Why inputs get ignored.**
- *Copycat agents* (Wen et al., [arXiv 2010.14876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14876)) and causal confusion (de Haan et al. 2019): a
  policy uses whichever input is easiest; an input that duplicates other information is dropped or copied. This is the mechanism
  behind batch 1, and why section 2 measures predictability first.

## 4. Design (user ideas → v9 pieces)

| User idea | v9 piece | Information / data | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| progress implicit, not an input | progress only predicted (9 action dims) | — | G4, G5 |
| better progress, flat when stuck | honest progress: nearest demo frames' road progress | — | G4 (gain AUC ≥ 0.75) |
| relabel the eval trials, longer stall → less progress, then RA-BC | `rabc` on the time-decayed progress (−0.3 stage per second standing still) | data yes (good share 48% → 6% as stalls grow) | offline ranking |
| learn the progress of failures, not their actions | `negmask` | — | G3 |
| progress sign controls the action | `adv` good / bad indicator + guidance | yes / yes (AUC 0.74, mixed labels) | G1 |
| supervisor: when stuck, go back and re-align | `advback` (learned), `STALL=rewind` (scripted), `STALL=learned` | yes / yes (AUC 0.47, 58 segments) | G2 |
| feed back the change of the increments (stall time) | not in the model (8 recoveries); `STALL=ask` collects recoveries first | data no | later |
| resampling gets it through | `best_of_n` by own progress | — | G5 |

## 5. Pre-registered expectations (written before any v9 result)

| Model | Expected | Would falsify it |
|---|---|---|
| `prog` | stall-frame close share 0.10–0.20 (more coverage of stall states than v6); G5 passes (corr ≤ −0.2) | close share below the v6 baseline's 0.112 |
| `adv` | G1 passes at w = 1 or 2 with effect / noise 3–10; close(good) − close(bad) ≈ 0.1–0.3 | effect / noise < 3: the indicator is ignored like batch-1 progress → stop at 60K |
| `adv_r2` | offline numbers within ±0.03 of `adv` | larger: offline differences between models smaller than that are noise |
| `negmask` | progress error on held-out failure frames below `prog`; close share ≈ `prog` | worse progress than `prog`: masking hurts more than it teaches |
| `rabc` | close share ≈ `prog` ± 0.03; fewer long stalls in closed loop (robot only) | — (weak expectation; RA-BC tied before) |
| `advback` | G2 passes: back moves the A arm toward its start pose in ≥ 70% of samples | toward share < 0.7 or effect / noise < 3 |

Robot statistics: 30 trials give about ±17 points on the success rate, so robot comparisons use the A对位 grasp rate and paired
tape positions; only models that pass their gates go to the robot.
